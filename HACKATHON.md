# Hackathon record

This repository is our entry to the Meta Global AI Developer Hackathon 2026, Startup track.

**Theme: Build for Your World.** The small shops we work with at airi.chat sell on WhatsApp and have no product photos. Their owners film on their phones and talk in their own language, so the import takes a narration in any language ElevenLabs Scribe transcribes, with each price in the currency the owner names: a shop owner anywhere can use it.

## In production

The Products section has run in production on [airi.chat](https://airi.chat) since Oct 29, 2026, behind the `products` flag. Its first import in production, our own 31-second test video sent over WhatsApp on Oct 30, stopped after 2 of its 5 photos: the whole video was one AI action, and every plan caps an action's spend at $0.50. The fix, which makes each photo its own AI action, went live on Nov 1; its row in "Features built" below is `meta-ai-hackathon: per-photo AI actions, phone approval, cinematic stage`. The change for shop owners anywhere went live on Nov 2: a narration in any language Scribe transcribes and each price in its own currency, with photos that no longer stop at the import's own reservations. Its rows in "Features built" below are `meta-ai-hackathon: make the photos an import's own reservations crowded out` and `meta-ai-hackathon: prices in any currency, narration in any language`.

## Try it

**The live product** is the Products section of [airi.chat](https://airi.chat), behind the `products` flag. Access details for judges are in our submission form, not in this repository.

These steps are for studio v3, live on airi.chat since Nov 4.

1. **Film and upload.** Film the way a shop owner would: hold each product up and say its name and price in your own language, and name the currency at least once (for example "twelve dollars fifty"). On the Products page, tap the **+** at the end of the shelf, or drop the video on the page; about a minute is plenty, and up to 5 minutes is taken. ElevenLabs Scribe v2 works out the language by itself; we have tested Kazakh on real recordings, and English and Japanese on synthetic voice-overs of our Kazakh shelf video ([docs/findings.md](docs/findings.md)). Each price keeps the currency you said or showed, and its cents. A currency word several countries share is read as its default currency unless the video points to another ("dollars" as USD), and a price whose currency was never said or shown is flagged for you to set.
2. **Watch the import** on its light stage: the video's first frame, five steps that each name who does them (Airi prepares the video, ElevenLabs Scribe v2 listens, Muse Spark 1.3 watches, Muse Spark, SAM 3.1 and Muse Image 1.0 make the photos, and you check), and "What you said": your own words for each product, with the name and price made of them and whether its photo is made.
3. **Pick.** The video becomes a row on the shelf, with its first 3 products picked for you. Tap a product to pick or unpick it; a picked product shows its number. The ad takes picks 1 to 3, and the WhatsApp chat tile up to 10. **Check one by one**, in the video's menu, opens the swipe-through review: right (or →) puts a product on the shelf, left (or ←) skips it, and a tap on its name or price fixes it.
4. **Make.** Drop your picks on a template tile, or tap a tile. The tiles are grouped by where they go: Reel, Story and Post (Instagram), WhatsApp ad (a Facebook ad that opens a WhatsApp chat) and WhatsApp chat. The first video, picture or WhatsApp-ad tile makes the ad, as one AI action: Muse Spark writes the words in the language you spoke, Muse Image places your products in scenes, SAM 3.1 frames them, and code draws the prices. In production on Nov 3 an ad took about a minute and a half. Every other tile for the same picks reuses that ad, at no cost. In the workbench, **Shots**, **Look** (your exact photo, an earlier picture, or a **New place** from Muse Image) and **Voice** (your recording, your voice reading new words after a consent tick, an Airi voice, or none) change the result, and picks wait until you press **Update video** once. Video ads draw Latin and Cyrillic letters only for now.
5. **The Meta action.** Each workbench has one button: **Post Reel**, **Post Story**, **Post** or **Post carousel** to Instagram, **Create ad** for a Click-to-WhatsApp ad (created paused, on Facebook placements), or **Create on WhatsApp** for the carousel and order form. The judge workspace has no Instagram account, Meta Ads account or WhatsApp number, so there they read **Connect Instagram**, **Connect Meta Ads** and **Connect WhatsApp**. Please don't connect your own accounts: the demo video shows each action on our own workspace.
6. **Out on Meta** lists what went out, each with its status and link. It stays empty in the judge workspace.

With WhatsApp on your phone, "Send from WhatsApp", in the menu of the **+** at the end of the shelf, links the phone to the workspace: scan the QR, send the message it writes, then confirm the phone on the page. Shelf videos you send to Airi after that are imported there too (after half an hour without one, Airi asks first).

## Known limits

- **The Voice Isolator only turns the background down.** When the video ad's voice is the owner's own recording, in our two test videos the ElevenLabs Voice Isolator lowered the background by about 5 dB at most: QazOner's music still plays under its presenter's words. The ad studio says the background is "turned down", not taken out ([docs/findings.md](docs/findings.md)).
- **Muse Image places products differently each time.** With the same prompt and cut-out, it put the top of the deer figure on QazOner's dancing table at 30% of the picture's height in one run and at 11% in another, where the ad's paper label covers it. The label stays legible, Muse Spark checks that every scene still shows the same product, and prices are never part of a picture, but part of a product can be covered.
- **Each press that makes the order form publishes a new WhatsApp Flow.** Meta lets a published Flow be deprecated, not deleted. The same press sent twice, or **Try again** after a lost answer, reuses what Meta already has.
- **Video ads draw Latin and Cyrillic letters only** (Kazakh's included), so a product named in another script can't go in one, though the import itself takes any language Scribe transcribes.
- **The Click-to-WhatsApp ad is created paused, on Facebook placements only** (studio v3). The campaign, ad set and ad are created `PAUSED` (the video and the creative have no on/off of their own), so nothing is spent; Meta reviews an ad before it can run ([Meta's Advertising Standards](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/)). It targets Kazakhstan, and the Meta Ads token can't reach Instagram placements. It needs the shop's WhatsApp number linked to its Facebook Page; until then the page says so. **Run** stays off unless the server's environment turns it on, and the server caps it at $1 a day for 3 days whatever the page sends. We have never pressed it.
- **Posting needs the shop's own accounts.** Instagram posts go to the Instagram account connected to the workspace, and Instagram limits how many it takes a day. The judge workspace has none of these connected, so judges see the **Connect** states, and our own posts and ads are shown in the demo video.
- **Not built yet:** the carousel's order button opening the order form, and a shop QR code.

## What surprised us

- **One box from Muse Spark is enough for SAM 3.1.** Muse Spark picks a frame and boxes the product once, SAM 3.1 tracks that one object through the product's clip, and code chooses the frame by measuring every mask: 7 of 7 clean cut-outs at confidence 0.98–0.99, where cutting from the frame sharpest overall gave 4–5 of 7 ([docs/findings.md](docs/findings.md)).
- **Muse Image fills in what the camera never saw.** Given room around a product the frame cut off, it completes the missing end to match the other, and it writes in lettering the video hid: Muse Spark reads what shows, and a name that was hidden comes back as a default full name, which the owner is told about. On QazOner's promo, shown with QazOner's permission, the medal holder, turned level from 37° in code first, came back from Muse Image with its cut-off end completed and a default name where a price sticker hid the buyer's (`meta-ai-hackathon: promo-quality product photos`).
- **Muse Spark makes a good checker of the other models' work.** It confirms each crop shows the product before Muse Image restores it, agrees that a levelled photo looks right, and checks that the final cut-out is still the same product, counting missing parts as a difference; that check caught a toy phone that had lost parts (`meta-ai-hackathon: handle videos that don't follow the brief`, `meta-ai-hackathon: products with options, level and see-through photos`, `meta-ai-hackathon: pipeline fixes from review`). The video ad checks each Muse Image scene the same way: in our paid test, Muse Spark rejected a scene of QazOner's family-tree panel that had lost the panel's outer frame, and the retry, told what was missing, passed (`meta-ai-hackathon: a video ad from the shelf, made with Muse Spark, Muse Image and SAM`, [docs/findings.md](docs/findings.md)).
- **Muse Spark Open matched a buyer's photo in one call.** Given a buyer's photo and a numbered sheet of the shop's cut-outs, the open-weight model recognised the doll in one call. A browser test found it once named the sheet's internal label ("товар 5") to the buyer; label mentions are now checked ([docs/findings.md](docs/findings.md)).
- **Muse Spark sees reliably but hears unreliably.** On our Kazakh-narrated shelf video it listed the same 7 products in every run, but the same file gave a Kazakh transcript with a price for every product once, an invented Russian narration another time, and "NO SPEECH" in others. It heard nothing in 5 of our first 9 attempts, none of which got the 5,000-tenge doll's price right (in 3 later speech-harness runs it heard the narration once, getting the doll right but the rooster wrong), and it reported the same input tokens either way, so a reply can't show whether the audio was used. That is why ElevenLabs Scribe listens ([docs/findings.md](docs/findings.md)).

## Meta models used

Apart from speech and voices, every AI model in the Products section is Meta's. ElevenLabs does those: Scribe transcribes the narration, and for the video ad the Voice Isolator turns down the background behind the owner's own recording. Since studio v2 (live on Nov 3), ElevenLabs text-to-speech can also read the ad's words in one of Airi's stock voices, or in an Instant Voice Clone of the owner, made from the import's narration only after the owner ticks consent. ffmpeg renders the video. The live product calls Meta's models through airi's Meta Model API client: `airi/packages/airi-runtime/src/meta-model-api.ts` (Muse Spark, Muse Image, file upload) and `meta-sam.ts` (SAM 3.1), with timeouts, retries and the cost of every call for airi's AI spend limits. Its pipeline is in `airi/apps/temporal-worker/src/media/product-video/`, written `product-video/` below, and the video ad's is in `product-video/ad/`.

**Built with Muse Spark Open.** The prototype's AI seller is built with Muse Spark Open. Its licence ([section 2 on the model card](https://huggingface.co/meta-models/Muse-Spark-Open-816B-A42B)) asks for that notice in the product's interface or its documentation, so it is here, in the [README](README.md), and under the title of the prototype's shop chat. Muse Spark Open isn't part of airi.chat.

**Speech is not a Meta model.** ElevenLabs Scribe v2 transcribes the owner's narration, with a time for every word. Muse Voice Transcribe has no Russian or Kazakh, and Muse Spark and Muse Spark Open missed spoken prices in most of our test runs, while Scribe got all 7 prices in every scored run ([docs/findings.md](docs/findings.md)). Meta's own model docs say audio understanding in Muse Spark 1.3 is not fully supported ([dev.meta.ai/docs/models](https://dev.meta.ai/docs/models)), and the 25 languages of Muse Voice Transcribe include neither Russian nor Kazakh ([dev.meta.ai/docs/speech-to-text](https://dev.meta.ai/docs/speech-to-text)). We tried Muse Voice Transcribe once anyway, on the English dub of the shelf video: it heard all 7 prices and wrote 6 right, for $0.003, but it gives times per phrase, not per word, which the import needs to match each price to its moment on screen. ElevenLabs lists 90+ languages for Scribe v2, and no language is set: Scribe works out which one is spoken, so owners can narrate in their own. In airi.chat it runs through airi's existing ElevenLabs client, called from `airi/apps/temporal-worker/src/activities/product-video-import.ts`; in the prototype, from `packages/catalog/src/speech.ts`. The Products page says so too: its Listen step names ElevenLabs Scribe v2. For the video ad's default voice, the owner's own words are cut from their video: the ElevenLabs Voice Isolator, through a new client in airi's runtime (`airi/packages/airi-runtime/src/elevenlabs-audio-isolation.ts`), turns the background down, and ffmpeg evens the voice (`product-video/ad/audio.ts`). Studio v2 (live in airi.chat since Nov 3 and ported in `airi/`) adds synthesised voices: ElevenLabs text-to-speech reads new words in Airi's stock voices, or in the owner's own voice through an ElevenLabs Instant Voice Clone made after the owner ticks consent, and both are booked in the ad's one AI action. Prices in those words are written out by code from the shelf, never by a model.
