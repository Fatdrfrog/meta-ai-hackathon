# Hackathon record

This repository is our entry to the Meta Global AI Developer Hackathon 2026, Startup track.

**Theme: Build for Your World.** The small shops we work with at airi.chat sell on WhatsApp and have no product photos. Their owners film on their phones and talk in their own language, so the import takes a narration in any language ElevenLabs Scribe transcribes, with each price in the currency the owner names: a shop owner anywhere can use it.

## In production

The Products section has run in production on [airi.chat](https://airi.chat) since Oct 29, 2026, behind the `products` flag. Its first import in production, our own 31-second test video sent over WhatsApp on Oct 30, stopped after 2 of its 5 photos: the whole video was one AI action, and every plan caps an action's spend at $0.50. The fix, which makes each photo its own AI action, went live on Nov 1; its row in "Features built" below is `meta-ai-hackathon: per-photo AI actions, phone approval, cinematic stage`. The change for shop owners anywhere went live on Nov 2: a narration in any language Scribe transcribes and each price in its own currency, with photos that no longer stop at the import's own reservations. Its rows in "Features built" below are `meta-ai-hackathon: make the photos an import's own reservations crowded out` and `meta-ai-hackathon: prices in any currency, narration in any language`.

airi.chat's production code also has three small follow-ups to the import that this repository leaves out: the private commits `84da52075`, `d87b9439b` and `26d38a348`, which test the import against airi.chat's newer rules for admitting AI actions, price its transcription from a shared Scribe price list, and settle a failed transcription by what ElevenLabs billed. They build on spend-accounting changes airi.chat made outside this project, which this copy of airi doesn't have. So here the import keeps its own Scribe rate, and a failed transcription is settled by the import's general rule for failed calls. And since this copy's allowance doesn't report the spend left, its photo passes can't stop once less than a photo's $0.15 reservation is left, as airi.chat's do; they end at the next pass that makes no photo.

## Try it

**The live product** is the Products section of [airi.chat](https://airi.chat), behind the `products` flag. Access details for judges are in our submission form, not in this repository. There you can:

- upload a short shelf video, about a minute long, on the Products page, or drop it on the page. Film it the way a shop owner would: hold each product up and say its name and price in your own language, and name the currency at least once (for example "twelve dollars fifty"). ElevenLabs Scribe v2 works out the language by itself; we have tested Kazakh on real recordings, and English and Japanese on synthetic voice-overs of our Kazakh shelf video ([docs/findings.md](docs/findings.md)). Each price keeps the currency you said or showed, and its cents. A price whose currency was never said or shown is flagged on its card for you to set;
- watch the import live: each step names the model doing it, and each product's photo appears as it's made, with the words its price came from;
- check what the video found as a card stack: swipe right (or →) to put a product on the shelf, left (or ←) to skip it, or tap its name or price to fix it (a price takes its amount and a 3-letter currency code, such as USD);
- see the shelf: the products you added, standing as cut-outs with their price tags.

With WhatsApp on your phone, "Send from WhatsApp" on the Products page links the phone to the workspace: scan the QR, send the message it writes, then confirm the phone on the page. Shelf videos you send to Airi after that are imported there too (after half an hour without one, Airi asks first). The carousel and order-form drafts need a WhatsApp number connected to the workspace; without one, the demo video shows them.

**The 3-minute demo video**, linked from our submission, shows the whole flow, WhatsApp included: a video sent to Airi, the products it found, and the carousel and order form made from them for buyers.

**The prototype**, with the AI seller on Muse Spark Open, runs locally from this repository: see [Run the prototype](README.md#run-the-prototype). Upload a shelf video in its studio, confirm the products, then ask the seller about them in the shop page's chat. It needs your own Meta Model API, ElevenLabs and Baseten keys; [Setup](README.md#setup) says which Baseten model ID to use. The prototype is the first version and expects prices in tenge.

## What surprised us

- **One box from Muse Spark is enough for SAM 3.1.** Muse Spark picks a frame and boxes the product once, SAM 3.1 tracks that one object through the product's clip, and code chooses the frame by measuring every mask: 7 of 7 clean cut-outs at confidence 0.98–0.99, where cutting from the frame sharpest overall gave 4–5 of 7 ([docs/findings.md](docs/findings.md)).
- **Muse Image fills in what the camera never saw.** Given room around a product the frame cut off, it completes the missing end to match the other, and it writes in lettering the video hid: Muse Spark reads what shows, and a name that was hidden comes back as a default full name, which the owner is told about. On QazOner's promo, shown with QazOner's permission, the medal holder, turned level from 37° in code first, came back from Muse Image with its cut-off end completed and a default name where a price sticker hid the buyer's (`meta-ai-hackathon: promo-quality product photos`).
- **Muse Spark makes a good checker of the other models' work.** It confirms each crop shows the product before Muse Image restores it, agrees that a levelled photo looks right, and checks that the final cut-out is still the same product, counting missing parts as a difference; that check caught a toy phone that had lost parts (`meta-ai-hackathon: handle videos that don't follow the brief`, `meta-ai-hackathon: products with options, level and see-through photos`, `meta-ai-hackathon: pipeline fixes from review`).
- **Muse Spark Open matched a buyer's photo in one call.** Given a buyer's photo and a numbered sheet of the shop's cut-outs, the open-weight model recognised the doll in one call. A browser test found it once named the sheet's internal label ("товар 5") to the buyer; label mentions are now checked ([docs/findings.md](docs/findings.md)).
- **Muse Spark sees reliably but hears unreliably.** On our Kazakh-narrated shelf video it listed the same 7 products in every run, but the same file gave a Kazakh transcript with a price for every product once, an invented Russian narration another time, and "NO SPEECH" in others. It heard nothing in 5 of our first 9 attempts, none of which got the 5,000-tenge doll's price right (in 3 later speech-harness runs it heard the narration once, getting the doll right but the rooster wrong), and it reported the same input tokens either way, so a reply can't show whether the audio was used. That is why ElevenLabs Scribe listens ([docs/findings.md](docs/findings.md)).

**Which Muse Spark Open ran.** The code asks Baseten for the hackathon's model ID, `meta-models/muse-spark-open-1026`, unless `BASETEN_MODEL` says otherwise. Baseten serves that ID only to accounts whose hackathon credit request it has approved, and ours hadn't been approved: the ID answered 404. So every seller run so far, the 9 of 9 evaluation and the shop-chat tests included, used Baseten's public ID for Muse Spark Open, `meta-models/muse-spark-open`, set in our `.env`.

**Speech is not a Meta model.** ElevenLabs Scribe v2 transcribes the owner's narration, with a time for every word. Muse Voice Transcribe has no Russian or Kazakh, and Muse Spark and Muse Spark Open missed spoken prices in most of our test runs, while Scribe got all 7 prices in every scored run ([docs/findings.md](docs/findings.md)). ElevenLabs lists 90+ languages for Scribe v2, and no language is set: Scribe works out which one is spoken, so owners can narrate in their own. In airi.chat it runs through airi's existing ElevenLabs client, called from `airi/apps/temporal-worker/src/activities/product-video-import.ts`; in the prototype, from `packages/catalog/src/speech.ts`. The Products page says so too: its Listen step names ElevenLabs Scribe v2.
